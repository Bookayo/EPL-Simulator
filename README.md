# Premier League Simulator
#### Video Demo:  <https://youtu.be/c33uuzJS_UY>
#### Description:
Premier League Simulator is a program that allows users to simulate through up to 10 seasons of the English Premier League football where they can control the results of one selected team.

In this program, the first thing it does is output the initial Premier League table before the start of the 2024/25 season sorted alphabetically. This table consists of team names, matches played, games won (W), games drawn (D), games lost (L), goals for (GF), goals against (GA), goals difference (GD) and total points. The users then have to select a Premier League team to start simulating from the 2024/25 season. For each matchday, the program will output the fixtures with the selected team in bold. The users then have to decide if their team will win, draw, or lose against another team. It is recommended that the decision is thorough and logical so that the simulation is realistic (for example, a newly promoted team that is stuck in the last place is unlikely to beat the top teams competing for the title), but, ultimately, the decision is completely up to the users. After the users input the result, the program will generate the scores for that match and realistically generate the results and scores for the other 9 matches. It then outputs the results and the scores along with the updated league table so the users can see how every team is performing. The cycle then repeats for next matchdays with different fixtures. And just like the actual Premier League, there are 38 matchdays in total with every team playing each other twice, at home and away. At the end of the season, the final league table shows where each team has finished and how they performed. If the selected team finishes in the bottom three, then it is relegated from the Premier League and the simulation is over. Otherwise, the users have the option to end the simulation there or continue to the next season. If the users choose to proceed, the program will also replace the three relegated teams with three random teams from the EFL Championship before the start of new season; the three relegated teams will also have a chance to return in the future seasons if they are randomly picked. The program will then output the initial league table before the start of the 2025/26 season sorted alphabetically with the three newly promoted teams. And, you guessed it, the whole process repeats! Without relegation, the users can simulate up until the 2033/34 season (10 seasons in total) to see how each team performs in the long term, which teams are relegated, which teams are promoted and how they perform in the Premier League. Whenever the simulation has ended, the program will output an appropriate message and, if any, the total number of titles won by the selected team.

In terms of programming, the whole program is done only on project.py. Tabulate is the only pip-installable library used, it is used to output the league table. I have colour coded some important positions: first place in gold, relegation zone in red, and European competitions spots in blue, purple, and green (though they are meaningless in this simulation, just for extra realism). For the fixtures, I use the 2024/25 Premier League fixtures, but instead of team names, I use their index in the Premier League array that I made, so they can be repeated over many seasons with different teams. I also made another array that stores all of the 2024/25 Championship teams so the three promoted teams can be randomly picked from here and the three relegated teams can also be stored here for future uses. It may be useful to note that I only make and update the transitions between the Premier League and the Championship so there will never be teams from the lower leagues (e.g. League One and League Two) appearing in this simulation. The random module is used to generate the results and scores. It can be said that the only thing that is completely randomised is actually when picking three teams from the Championship array, there are no considerations for that, just pure luck. To generate a result, I assign a number to each team that represents the strength of it, with the higher number being stronger. So when two teams are playing against each other, I use the random module to randomise a number in the range of its strength for each team and compare them to determine the winner or declare a draw if both the generated numbers are the same. What I noticed later on was that there were not many draws compared to the real game because it is not often for the generated numbers, especially the ones from larger ranges, to be the same. So I added one more condition that would satisfy a draw to increase the chance of getting it. For the scores, most of the time the winner will not score more than 3 goals. However, there is a chance that the winner will score more than 3 goals when top teams play against teams at the lower end of the table, especially when they play at home. I also use the difference between the strengths of the two teams playing each other to help generate the scores. By using this method, where there is some form of randomisation built into the program, there will be unexpected results and performances, which is not a bad thing at all because they add fun, they happen in real world, and they are the beauty of football, I just hope that there will not be too many of them to the point where it is not believable and not enjoyable to the users. In the end, the only thing that can be controlled are the results of the team selected by the users.
